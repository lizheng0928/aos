3.2 Legal: Supplier Contract Review, Template Generation & Expiry Tracking

Corporate legal work isn't legal research — it's managing contracts. Supplier T&Cs need comparing against company standard terms, NDA and distribution agreements are copied from old documents, and contract expiry dates are scattered across emails and folders. These three tasks take up most of the legal team's time, and each one is a perfect fit for AOS Work — using your own documents and data.

Sub-scenario A: Supplier Contract Review — Compare Against Standards, Report in Minutes

Pain Point

"Supplier sends a 25-page T&C. I need to compare it line by line against our company standard terms. One contract takes 2 hours. Three contracts, and the day is gone."

Steps

1.

Prepare standard terms: Save your company's standard contract terms to Library (PDF or Word)

2.

Upload supplier contract: Upload the supplier's T&C to the conversation or Library

3.

Instruct the Agent to compare: Tell the Agent which documents are which, ask for a line-by-line comparison

4.

Review the report: The Agent outputs a difference report. Legal reviews and confirms.

Task Instruction

Compare the following two contracts and output a difference report: Document 1 (Company Standard Terms): Library / Legal/Standard_Terms.pdf Document 2 (Supplier T&C): Uploaded file Supplier_Terms.pdf Comparison dimensions: 1. Payment terms (terms, currency, penalties) 2. IP ownership 3. Confidentiality (scope, duration) 4. Termination (conditions, notice period) 5. Liability limitations 6. Governing law and dispute resolution For each dimension: - Mark differences (added / removed / modified) - Assign risk level (High / Medium / Low) - Provide 3 key negotiation suggestions Output: Generate a Word difference report, clearly formatted and editable — legal team can annotate and modify directly. Also log this review in the "Contract Review Records" Base.

Expected Results

•

Difference comparison table across 6 dimensions

•

Risk level assigned to each difference

•

3 key negotiation suggestions

•

Difference report as a Word document, editable by legal, plus logged in the contract review table

💡 The Agent does document comparison, not legal advice. Risk levels are based on clause differences. Final acceptance is up to the legal team. The Agent saves the line-by-line comparison time — it doesn't replace legal judgment.

Sub-scenario B: Contract Template Generation — NDA & Distribution Agreements from One Brief

Pain Point

"Every time we sign an NDA, I copy from the last one, change the company name, date, and clauses, then check the formatting. Distribution agreements are more complex — different clause combinations. One document takes 40 minutes. I do dozens a month."

Steps

1.

Prepare templates: Save company standard NDA and distribution agreement templates to Library

2.

Fill in parameters: Tell the Agent what type of agreement, counterparty info, and key terms

3.

Generate document: The Agent fills the template and generates the complete document

4.

Review and archive: Legal reviews, saves to Library, and logs in the table

Task Instruction

Generate NDA from Library template: Template: Library / Legal/Templates/NDA_Template.docx Agreement type: Mutual NDA Counterparty info: - Company: [Counterparty Name] - Registered in: [City, Country] - Signatory: [Name, Title] Key terms: - Confidentiality period: 3 years - Governing law: Italian law - Dispute resolution: Milan arbitration - Confidential scope: Technical data, business plans, customer data - Exclusions: Already public information, lawfully obtained from third parties Output: Word document, preserve template formatting, replace all placeholders. Constraint: Do not add clauses not in the template, do not modify standard clause wording.

Expected Results

•

A complete NDA Word document, ready to send to the counterparty

•

All placeholders replaced, template formatting preserved

•

A record created in the "Contract Register" Base (contract name, counterparty, type, signing date, expiry date)

💡 The Agent only fills parameters from the template — it won't write its own clauses. If a clause isn't in the template, the Agent will say "corresponding clause not found in template." Legal only needs to verify the parameters, not write from scratch.

Sub-scenario C: Contract Expiry & Renewal Tracking — Auto-Remind Before the Window Closes

Pain Point

"Last year's supplier agreement auto-renewed because we forgot to send the termination notice 60 days before. This happens 2–3 times a year, and we have to renegotiate every time."

Steps

1.

Build the contract register: Create a Base recording each contract's key info

2.

Set expiry reminders: Use a scheduled task to scan the register and auto-remind before expiry

3.

Track renewal status: Record renewal progress in the table (notified, negotiating, renewed, terminated)

Scheduled Task Instruction

Run on the 1st of each month at 9:00 AM: - Query the "Contract Register" Base - Filter: Expiry date within next 30 days AND renewal status = "Active" - Output: Contracts expiring soon - Contract name, Counterparty, Expiry date, Days remaining, Auto-renewal, Notice deadline - Write to "Expiry Reminder" view in the table - Also output summary in conversation

💡 The scheduled task runs monthly. Legal opens AOS Work on the first working day and sees which contracts are expiring in the next 30 days. No longer hand-digging through Excel or emails.

Summary

In short: Agent handles contract review and expiry tracking; you focus on legal decisions.
