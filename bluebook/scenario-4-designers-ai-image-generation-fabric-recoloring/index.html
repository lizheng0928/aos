3.4 Designers: AI Image Generation for Fashion

A designer's work combines creative vision with hands-on execution — finding inspiration, sketching, color-matching, building lookbooks. This chapter shows you how to hand the repetitive visual work to the Agent: preview sketches on different fabrics before sampling, recolor flat sketches, and let designers spend their time on creative decisions.

The Designer Workflow

Whether you're a fashion designer or a visual designer, the daily workflow is pretty much like this:

Step

Traditional approach

Pain point

1. Moodboard collection

Browse Pinterest, Instagram — manually screenshot

Manual browsing is slow, and images are scattered around, not easy to find and compare

2. Sketching

Hand-drawn or CAD software, one by one

Every colorway needs a separate drawing

3. Color confirmation

Sample → photo → compare → approve

2–3 week cycle, back-and-forth communication

4. Flat lays / Lookbooks

Studio shoot or 3D render

Tight schedule, high cost, too many SKUs

With AOS Work, steps 1–4 can be handled by the OpenAI Image Generation Connector or Nano Banana Image Generator, depending on the model you want to use. Output goes directly to Library, linked to the Base.

Sketch to Fabric Preview — See How It Looks Before Sampling

Pain Point

After finishing a sketch, designers want to see how it looks in different fabrics. Traditionally, this means waiting for samples — a 2–3 week cycle. By the time the sample arrives, the design direction may have shifted, or the fabric is out of season. Being able to preview how a sketch looks on different fabrics — before committing to sampling — is critical for fast iteration.

Steps

1.

Upload the sketch: Upload the line art sketch to the conversation or Library

2.

Select fabric: Upload fabric photos to Library, or describe the fabric in natural language (e.g., "heavyweight cotton twill, stone-washed finish")

3.

Generate preview: In the conversation, click the "+" menu, choose Connector, and select OpenAI Image Generation or Nano Banana depending on your preferred model. The Agent applies the fabric to the sketch, preserving all design details

4.

Try color variations: Using the same fabric, generate previews in different colors to compare (e.g., navy vs. olive vs. black)

5.

Save and tag: Output PNGs are saved to Library with tags (sketch ID, fabric type, color, season)

Task Instruction

Apply this sketch to the following fabrics and generate flat lay previews: 1. Heavyweight cotton twill, stone-washed (upload fabric photo from Library) 2. Lightweight linen, natural (describe: "lightweight linen, natural beige tone") For each fabric, generate 3 color variations: navy, olive, black. Save all outputs to Library folder "2026SS/Previews", tag with: sketch=SK-2026-042, season=2026SS.

Expected Results

•

2 fabrics × 3 colors = 6 preview images

•

All saved to Library with consistent tags

•

Design decisions made before sampling — no waiting for physical samples

💡 Tips

Brand assets in Library: Save your brand logo and design assets to Library in advance. When generating images, the AI can directly use your brand design library — no manual downloading or image editing. Output specifications: You can specify output requirements in your prompt — PNG or JPG format, resolution, dimensions, etc. The AI will generate images to your exact specifications.

Extra Tip

Want the team to review and approve final designs? Create a Base table to track design status:

•

Designing — sketch in progress

•

Design Approved — team has reviewed and approved

•

Sampling — sent to factory for sample

•

Sample Received — physical sample arrived for final check

Link each preview image to its corresponding record, so the team can see the full history from sketch to sample.

Set permissions for collaboration: Share the Base with your team and assign roles:

•

Admin — design leads can manage the table, update status, approve designs

•

Reader — team members (merchandisers, product managers) can view progress and leave feedback, but can't modify records

This way, everyone stays aligned on where each design stands — without endless email chains or status meetings.

What the Image Generation Connector Can Do

The Image Generation Connector (OpenAI Image Generation or Nano Banana) supports the following capabilities:

•

Text-to-image fashion concepts — Create original garments, outfits, models, poses, locations, lighting, and styling from a written description.

•

Product and e-commerce images — Studio shots, mannequin imagery, clean white-background product photos, editorial campaigns, and lookbook visuals.

•

Garment recoloring — Change colors while preserving the garment's shape, construction, and fabric texture.

•

Flat sketch to draped 3D rendering — Turn a technical flat or flat-lay garment reference into a realistic worn or draped version.

•

Garment to technical flat sketch — Convert a garment image into a clean front/back technical fashion drawing.

•

Studio model to lifestyle scene — Place a garment or model into settings such as a street, resort, office, studio, or editorial environment.

•

Upscaling and enhancement — Improve image quality, sharpness, presentation, and visual polish.

•

Half-body to full-body completion — Extend a partial model image when you provide a suitable full-body pose reference.

•

Reference-style generation — Use supplied reference images to guide the visual direction, such as lighting, composition, mood, or styling.

Example Requests

•

"Create a luxury campaign image of a black silk evening dress in a modern Parisian interior."

•

"Recolor this jacket from beige to deep burgundy while preserving the fabric texture."

•

"Turn this garment flat sketch into a realistic 3D fashion rendering on a model."

•

"Create five editorial outfit concepts inspired by minimalist Scandinavian tailoring."

•

"Convert this garment photo into a clean technical flat sketch."

•

"Place this studio product image in a natural outdoor lifestyle setting."

In short: Agent handles fabric previews and visual variations; you focus on creative direction.
